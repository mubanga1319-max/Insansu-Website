Projects & initiatives

Agro-processing · Northern & Luapula Provinces

Climate-Smart Indigenous Crops Production & Agro-processing Project

Status: Proposed · seeking partners and funding Duration: 24 months Location: Northern & Luapula Provinces, Zambia Output: Peanut butter & composite flours

This proposed project would build climate-smart production and agro-processing capacity for indigenous crops across Northern and Luapula Provinces. Smallholder growers would be supported through the production stage, with produce aggregated and processed into two flagship products: peanut butter and composite flours.

The project is designed to demonstrate, at real scale, how indigenous crops can move from field to finished, market-ready nutrition products — while keeping the economic benefit close to the communities that grow them.

Aggregation & processing · Coming soon

Insansu Kawambwa Aggregation & Processing Centre

Status: Coming soon Location: Plot 3861, New Town, Kawambwa, Luapula Province Role: Aggregation and agro-processing

Insansu is establishing a presence in Luapula and Northern Provinces, and Kawambwa will be its base there: a facility where produce from smallholder growers is aggregated and processed into finished products.

It is the processing link for the Climate-Smart Indigenous Crops project, bringing peanut butter, composite flours and other value-added products closer to where the crops are grown, so more of the value stays in the province.

Water & irrigation · Seeking funding

Insansu Solar-Powered Community Water & Irrigation Hub

Status: Seeking funding Sites: Kafue (Lusaka Province) · Kawambwa (Luapula Province) · Mungwi, Mpanda (Northern Province) Power source: Solar Aims: Year-round production and safe drinking water for homesteads

One model, three sites. The Hub brings solar-powered water to farming communities, keeping two lines fully separate: safe drinking water for homesteads, and drip irrigation for the fields. Households get a reliable tap at home and a productive plot, so they can grow high-value and indigenous vegetables through the whole year and not only in the rains.

The first site is at our farm in Chipongwe, Kafue, where we already grow under drip irrigation. The same model is planned for Kawambwa and for Mpanda Constituency in Mungwi District, where our survey found that most households draw water from wells. Final designs, scale and timing at each site depend on technical assessments and funding.

How the Hub works

The technique and layout.

Each site follows the same simple layout, built around a central water point and an anchor farm that the surrounding households connect to.

1. Solar-powered water source

A borehole with a solar pump supplies the Hub, so running costs stay low and pumping does not depend on the grid.

2. Storage and two separate lines

Water is held in elevated storage and sent along two lines: one for drinking, one for irrigation. Keeping them apart protects household water quality.

3. Household taps

Lead households each receive a tap, so families have safe drinking water close to the home and no longer rely on open wells.

4. Drip-irrigated plots

Drip lines deliver water at the roots of each plant, using less water and allowing growing through the dry season.

5. Anchor farm

A central anchor farm demonstrates good practice, supplies inputs and seedlings, and acts as the learning point for the Hub.

6. Farmers and market

Households grow to a common plan, sell through Insansu's aggregation, and mentor neighbouring farmers as the Hub grows.

Alongside the infrastructure we promote climate-smart practices such as mulching, farm-made compost and careful water management, so that more water and better soils turn into steadier harvests and income.

Seedlings & propagation · Starting up

Insansu Nursery

Status: Starting up Location: Chipongwe, Kafue

We are setting up a nursery to raise healthy seedlings for our own fields and for the smallholder growers we work with, including indigenous vegetables alongside cabbage, onion and tomato.

Starting crops from strong seedlings gives growers a more reliable harvest, and it ties directly into our Water & Irrigation Hub and the wider Insansu value chain.
